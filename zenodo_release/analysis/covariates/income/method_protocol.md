# Prespecified FY2015 income sensitivity: method and decision log

Status: optional sensitivity, separate from the frozen main analysis. This protocol was written before fitting the adjusted model. The frozen panel, coefficient file, manuscript, and analysis scripts must not be overwritten.

## Question and estimand

The unit is a **country-month** in the same 25-country, July 2014–December 2019 balanced panel. Let `Y_cm` indicate at least one dashboard-classified reactive OCV request in country `c`, month `m`. Let `A_cm` be the sum of active outbreak records across **months m−12 through m−1** (each record contributes one for each recorded active month, inclusive of start and end); `Z_cm = log(1 + A_cm)`. On the fixed 66-month estimation period, `B_c = mean_m(Z_cm)` and `W_cm = Z_cm − B_c`. Let `L_c = 1` for World Bank FY2015 low-income classification, and `0` for lower- or upper-middle-income classification.

Fit the pooled logistic model

`logit Pr(Y_cm = 1 | B_c, W_cm, L_c) = alpha + beta_B B_c + beta_W W_cm + gamma L_c`.

The sensitivity estimand is the conditional *country-month log-odds association* `beta_B`, comparing two countries differing by one unit in their 66-month mean recorded log activity **at the same FY2015 income category and the same monthly deviation**. `beta_W` compares months within the same country at the same mean activity; it is not a country-fixed-effects estimate. This distinction follows the general between/within warning of [Neuhaus & Kalbfleisch (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9629647/), though their model is not this pooled model. `gamma` is a descriptive contrast, not the causal effect of income. Keep predictor construction, outcome, sample, link, country clustering, and reporting scale identical to the frozen benchmark. Do not add interactions, calendar terms, other covariates, or alternative cutpoints in this sensitivity.

The income source is [World Bank, *Global Monitoring Report 2014/2015*, Appendix F, Table F.1, p. 220](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/Worldbank/gmr/gmr2014/GMR_2014_Appendices.pdf). Its FY2015 classification uses 2013 Atlas GNI per capita, preceding the panel's start. This is a coarse baseline development proxy, **not** an observed measure of cholera-surveillance, request-writing, delivery, or health-system capacity. The lookup and audit panel are documented in [README.md](README.md).

## Inferential specification and validation gates

Use the same `statsmodels` binomial GLM as the frozen implementation. Report beta on the log-odds scale and `exp(beta)` as an odds ratio; the CI is a normal 95% CI using a country-cluster sandwich with `use_correction=False`, matching the frozen calculation. For the two outbreak-activity coefficients only, calculate two-sided p-values using the **same frozen WCLR-C score-linearized restricted wild-cluster bootstrap** with 99,999 Rademacher draws and the same base-seed rule; adding income changes the model dimension and restricted nuisance fits, so the adjusted p-values are newly calculated and are not directly recycled. The bootstrap family and its small-cluster motivation are described by [MacKinnon, Nielsen & Webb (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07474938.2025.2515161). The bootstrap observed statistic uses the frozen implementation's CV1 finite-sample factor, whereas the reported asymptotic CI uses its uncorrected sandwich. Label this distinction explicitly. The income coefficient is descriptive; avoid making it an additional hypothesis test.

Before accepting estimates, assert: (1) exactly 1,650 unique country-months, 25 countries × 66 months, 58 reactive event-months; (2) no missing values or ambiguous FY2015 categories; (3) original panel columns equal the frozen input; (4) independently reconstruct the prior-12-month count directly from each recorded outbreak interval using `max(0, min(end, m−1) − max(start, m−12) + 1)` in integer months, then verify `Z = log1p(A)`, `B+W=Z`, constant `B` and mean-zero `W` within each country; (5) the unadjusted same-data GLM reproduces frozen main coefficients, CIs, and AIC to numerical tolerance; (6) both income groups contain countries and event-months; (7) design has full rank and no extreme condition number; (8) finite estimates/predictions and GLM convergence; and (9) the income and between predictor are not nearly collinear. If a gate fails, stop without reporting an adjusted coefficient.

Pre-report influence diagnostics may include one-country-deleted *descriptive* coefficients for `beta_B`, `beta_W` and `gamma`, with the same 66-month country means and no refitting of the frozen benchmark. These are stability checks, **not** a menu for selecting an exclusion set or a source of confirmatory p-values. Flag dominance by DR Congo, South Sudan, Nigeria, or either zero-event UMC; report the full range transparently.

## Interpretation boundaries

- The sensitivity answers whether the original between association remains on the same side of zero after conditioning on **one** baseline income proxy. It cannot determine whether “capacity explains” that association, decompose mechanisms, or establish a causal effect. Logistic odds ratios can change after adjustment even without confounding because of [noncollapsibility](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34225653/); do not compute a “percent explained” from coefficient attenuation.
- Between information comes from **25 countries**, not 1,650 independent country-level observations. There are only 58 reactive event-months, and some countries contribute none. Sparse-data bias, influential countries, and uncertain small-cluster inference remain possible; there is no universal safe events-per-variable threshold, and predictor correlations matter ([Courvoisier et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21411281/)).
- The two UMC countries have no reactive event-months. Pooling them with LMC was specified *a priori* to avoid an unsupported separate UMC coefficient, but their lack of events may still affect `gamma` and `beta_B` in the binary model. Do not interpret income-group coefficients as a monotonic income gradient.
- Recorded outbreak-months mix record count, recorded duration, concurrency, and surveillance quality; they do not measure cases, at-risk population, eligible outbreaks, or what decision-makers knew at the request date. A recorded zero does not prove no cholera. National-month aggregation masks geographic targeting and cannot link a particular request to an outbreak. A binary month also collapses multiple requests.
- The 66-month country mean uses the full retrospective panel, including later months relative to an early outcome, so this is descriptive decomposition, not real-time prediction. The prior-12-month component excludes the outcome month, but retrospective start/end records may incorporate later knowledge. The 2013 income proxy may poorly represent changing capacity through 2019. Outbreak burden, reporting, prior campaigns, access to partners, and stockpile processes may all co-determine observed activity and requests ([Maffioli & Lu, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12115386/); [Zheng et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35605949/)).

Decision rule: if validation/support/convergence gates pass, report this as **one** exploratory sensitivity alongside the unchanged frozen benchmark, regardless of whether its p-value or coefficient looks favorable. If they fail, report a no-go and the failure; do not search alternative covariates or cutpoints to rescue significance. Candidate health-expenditure and prior-OCV measures require their own temporality/missingness/measurement protocol and Wayne's approval before any additional model.
